# 07. User interface

## Camera control

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Orbit | LMB |
| Zoom | wheel |
| Pan | RMB |
| Automatic orbit | the "Camera orbit" checkbox |

Ten preset views (buttons `1`…`9` and `0`): overview, cutaway, front, fan, HP
compressor, combustor, turbine, nozzle and jet, the rear view (the camera sits
right at the edge of the jet cone — the gas comes towards the viewer, but the
engine is not entirely drowned in it as it would be directly on the axis), and
the contrail, which stands far enough back for the trail to have somewhere to
run. Views that show internal modules switch the cutaway on automatically.
Moving between views is a smooth interpolation of both position and look-at
point.

## Keyboard shortcuts

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Space` | Air flows |
| `C` | Casing cutaway |
| `X` | Transparent casings (x-ray) |
| `S` | Engine sound |
| `E` | Start / shut down the engine |
| `H` | Exhaust gas |
| `T` | Contrail |
| `1`…`9`, `0` | Preset views |

Both keyboard layouts are handled: `C`/`С`, `X`/`Ч`, `S`/`Ы`, `E`/`У`, `H`/`Р`,
`T`/`Е` are the same physical keys on a Russian layout.

The letters do not change with the interface language — only the explanations
around them are translated. The handler matches on the character produced, so
per-language mnemonics would mean extending the list above for each of them,
and Chinese and Japanese have no letter to be mnemonic with at all: input goes
through an IME. One layout for everyone also keeps muscle memory and
screenshots portable between languages.

## Interface language

A drop-down at the top of the panel selects one of eight languages: English,
Russian, Spanish, Chinese (Simplified), French, Portuguese (Brazilian), German
and Japanese. French is there on merit rather than reach — the prototype
CFM56-7B is a GE/Safran engine, and the French side is the primary source behind
half the reference data in [`docs/engines/`](engines/README.md).

The options are written as endonyms — `Русский`, `中文`, `日本語` — so the list
reads the same whatever language is current, and a reader looks for their own
script rather than for a translated language name. It is a native `<select>`,
which renders in the system font: that is what keeps Chinese and Japanese
legible without shipping a font, and the project has no external assets at all.

The choice is remembered in `localStorage` under `turbofan.lang`. On a first
visit with nothing stored, the browser's own language list is matched against
the eight, falling back to English — `pt-PT` lands on the Brazilian text and
`zh-TW` on the Simplified, on the grounds that a script the reader can mostly
follow beats English.

Switching is immediate and does not reload the page: the panel, the 3D module
labels, the view buttons, the station table, the generated contrail sentence and
an already-open module card are all re-rendered in place.

Numbers follow the language as well. Five of the eight write the decimal comma,
so a pressure reads `28,4 bar` in German and `28.4 bar` in English. Thousands
are deliberately left ungrouped: a T4 of 1604 °C would otherwise print as German
`1.604 °C`, which reads as a number with a decimal point. Unit symbols —
`°C`, `bar`, `kN`, `km`, `rpm`, `%`, `Pa`, `kg/m³` — are not translated in any
language, and neither are the instrument markings `N1`, `N2`, `T4`, `G` and
`ISA`.

## Looking inside

Three independent means, which can be combined.

### Casing cutaway

An angular sector is cut out of the shells by two clipping planes. The trick:
if a material is given `clipIntersection = true`, a fragment stays visible where
it falls inside **at least one** of the half-spaces. The union of two
half-spaces is therefore all of space minus the sector between them:

```js
clipA.normal.set(0, cos(a − π/2), sin(a − π/2));   // sector from a
clipB.normal.set(0, cos(b + π/2), sin(b + π/2));   // to b
material.clipIntersection = true;
```

The planes are assigned **only** to the shell materials
(`getShellMaterials()`) — the nacelle, the cowls, the casings, the flame tube.
Rotors, blades, shafts and accessories are not cut, which gives the classic
exhibition cutaway rather than a model sliced in half. The cut angle and its
rotation are set by sliders.

### Transparent casings

The same shell materials are switched to `transparent` with an opacity of 0.16
and depth writing turned off. It engages automatically when the flows are
switched on and the cutaway is off — otherwise the flow inside the engine would
not be visible.

### Exploded view

Each module has its own explode vector: the nacelle moves up, the core cowl and
the accessory gearbox move down, and the remaining modules spread out along the
axis. The slider sets the fraction of the full separation; the rotors keep
turning in sync throughout, because the common angle is assigned to the rotor
groups rather than to their parent modules.

## Module information

Hovering the mouse shows a tooltip with the module name; clicking opens a card
with a description: purpose, number of stages and blades, operating
temperatures, design features.

Both the name and the description come from the dictionary, keyed off the module
name (`module.fan.title`, `module.fan.info`), and are resolved at the moment the
card is shown rather than when the geometry is built. That is what lets a card
that is already open follow a change of language without being reopened.

Picking goes through invisible proxy cylinders rather than the real geometry —
see [architecture](01-architecture.md#performance). With the cutaway or the
transparent casings switched on, the shell proxies are excluded so that whatever
is underneath can be clicked.

Module labels (`CSS2DRenderer`) are toggled by a checkbox. They do not account
for occlusion: the label of an internal module shows through the nacelle.

## Instrument panel

| Instrument | What it shows |
|---|---|
| N1 | Actual low-pressure rotor speed, % |
| N2 | Actual high-pressure rotor speed, % |
| T4 | Gas temperature ahead of the turbine, °C |
| Thrust | Computed thrust, kN |

The instruments show **actual** values, not the command: when the throttle is
moved one can watch the rotor speeds chase it at different rates, and on
shutdown watch everything fall to zero. The status bar above the slider shows
the mode: SHUT DOWN, STARTING, RUNNING, SHUTDOWN · RUNDOWN.

The throttle slider is active only in the "run" mode — in the others it is
dimmed and locked.

Below the status bar sits the **time scale ×1 / ×4**. A start takes about 40 s
and a rundown about 35 s, as on a real engine; the speed-up saves waiting
through them in full. It multiplies the time step for the engine state machine
only, so the flow particles and the camera move at their normal pace while the
rotor speeds, temperature and sound run faster.

## Ambient conditions

A separate block moves the air around the engine: an altitude slider (0…12 km),
a deviation of the day from standard (−30…+30 °C), a relative humidity slider
(0…100 %, over water) and three quick buttons — 0 km parked, 3 km climb, 11 km
cruise, right at the tropopause. Beneath them the result of the computation in
six cells: temperature, pressure and density; dew point, vapour pressure and
humidity over ice.

Two of those cells refuse to show a number rather than show a meaningless one.
Perfectly dry air has no dew point — there is no temperature at which nothing
would condense — and above freezing there is no saturation over ice, because
there is no ice; both print a dash. The over-ice figure lights up above 100 %:
that is the threshold at which a contrail would persist rather than evaporate,
and it is the only reading here that exists for a task not yet done (BL-21).

The buttons and the sliders drive the same pair of numbers, so pressing a button
moves the sliders. A button lights up when the altitude matches it exactly,
whichever of the two set it.

What changes on screen is the station table in the flow legend: it is counted
off from the ambient air, and at eleven kilometres the whole pressure column
shrinks by a factor of four. The engine itself does not notice the altitude —
the speeds, T4 and thrust stay as they were; why that is so, and what it would
take to change it, is in [physics](03-physics.md#ambient-conditions).

Moving the sliders does not touch the engine, so the table would not be redrawn
by itself: the panel refresh is guarded by a hash of the engine state, and the
ambient controls invalidate that hash explicitly.

## Contrail

Its own block below the ambient conditions: the verdict on a status bar — NO
TRAIL, SHORT-LIVED or PERSISTENT — an efficiency slider, and three cells with
the slope of the mixing line, the threshold temperature and the temperature of
the two streams mixed at the nozzle.

The **efficiency** is a slider and not a computed value because the model has
neither the fuel flow nor the flight speed it would take to compute it. It earns
its place all the same: it is the control that shows the counter-intuitive part
of the physics, that a more efficient engine leaves a trail *more* readily.

Below them a line of prose says how far the conditions are from the threshold
and what would have to change — "the air is 7.0 °C below the threshold, so a
trail forms; it is supersaturated over ice, so the trail spreads instead of
evaporating. 1.4 km lower it would stop." The altitude in that sentence is found
by walking the criterion, not by dividing a temperature margin by the lapse
rate: pressure moves the threshold too.

The trail itself is drawn behind the engine and is switched off by a checkbox or
the `T` key. View `0` — the tenth — pulls the camera back far enough to see it,
and at that distance the module labels are hidden: they would otherwise collapse
into a heap of boxes over an engine a centimetre across. Below 45 units, which
is everything the camera could reach before this view existed, nothing changes.

## Flow legend

Appears together with the flow visualisation: a key to the colour scale and a
table of temperatures and pressures at the seven stations of the gas path,
recomputed in real time. Pressures below 10 bar are shown to two decimals: at
altitude the intake sits at 0.23 bar, and a single decimal would flatten the
whole column.
